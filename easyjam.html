Overview

EasyJam was made for an AI music-therapy program for adults with developmental disabilities at Mirae Therapy Center, selected for the 2024 Gyeonggi-do Integrated Support Program for Artists with Disabilities. In each session, participants play together with a therapist over a shared backing track.

Each participant picks an instrument on their own device: piano, saxophone, or two percussion sets. Whatever key or pad they press, EasyJam plays notes that fit the chord sounding at that moment, so people who have never learned an instrument can still take part in the ensemble.

The therapist runs the session from a console: starting and pausing the music, adjusting the tempo, setting a support level for each participant, and choosing whether everyone plays together, one person solos, or turns rotate every few bars.

All devices follow one server-synchronised clock, so the backing track and every instrument stay on the same beat. Percussion can also be played by moving the head in front of a camera.

Features

Chord-aware notes for any key or pad Therapist console Turn-taking: together, solo, rotating Support levels: easy, normal, challenge Camera-based head-movement percussion Synchronised playback across devices Session logs (CSV)
